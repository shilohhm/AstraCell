# Canonical figures

AstraCell writes 25 figures (all regenerated by the examples - see
[REPRODUCIBILITY.md](REPRODUCIBILITY.md) §3). These seven are the public evidence
sequence: one per beat of the argument, ordered as the case is made. The **★** four are
promoted to the README; all seven anchor [TECHNICAL_REPORT.md](TECHNICAL_REPORT.md).

Example 08 writes a 26th, `real_cell_capacity.png`, only when the Oxford dataset is present.
It is deliberately **not** in the canonical sequence: it carries no pinned result until a real
run produces one ([REAL_CELL.md](REAL_CELL.md), [CLAIMS.md](CLAIMS.md) C19).

All figures live in `reports/figures/`. Each caption below says what to look at and the
one command that regenerates it.

---

### 1. ★ The thesis - grey cells fall out of the bound

**`packmap_cooling.png`** · `python examples/01_first_demo.py`

A 32-cell pack shaded by whether a 40% cooling fault is identifiable. Exactly **4 of 32**
cells are diagnosable - precisely the four carrying a thermocouple. The grey is not
painted by a distance-to-sensor rule; it falls out of the Cramér-Rao bound. The fault on
cell 10 is *really there*, and AstraCell declines to diagnose it.

*Look at:* the four green cells sitting on the sensor positions (4, 12, 20, 28), and the
grey everywhere else.

### 2. Refusal is half an answer - the other half is what to measure

**`sensor_placement_before_after.png`** · `python examples/01_first_demo.py`

The blind cell 10 before and after a counterfactual intervention. Adding one thermocouple
moves its cooling-fault SNR from 1.24σ (REFUSE) to 5.71σ (DIAGNOSE), a 4.6× tighter
bound - computed as a row mask over the sensitivity tensor, with no re-simulation. A
harder current pulse (1.83C) achieves the same with no new hardware.

*Look at:* the verdict flipping from grey to green, and that excitation substitutes for a
sensor.

### 3. ★ The bound cannot see model bias

**`model_mismatch.png`** · `python examples/04_model_mismatch.py`

The central insight, made visual: as you replicate the experiment, the **reported**
(variance-only) confidence climbs without bound - to 14 611σ at 10 000 replicas - while
the **credible** confidence, which accounts for the observer's structural bias, sits
fixed at a ceiling of 6.49σ. More data buys precision, not accuracy.

*Look at:* the two diverging curves - one growing as √k, one flat.

### 4. ★ Are the verdicts calibrated?

**`calibration_coverage.png`** · `python examples/05_calibrated_abstention.py`

Empirical coverage against nominal confidence. Under a *matched* model the maximum-
likelihood interval tracks the diagonal - the first evidence in the repository that the
Cramér-Rao bound is *attained*, not merely asserted. Under *mismatch* the variance-only
interval covers the truth **essentially never**; the bias-aware interval restores coverage
only by becoming uselessly wide.

*Look at:* the matched curve on the diagonal, and the variance-only curve pinned at zero.

### 5. More samples cut variance, not bias

**`calibration_snr_vs_samples.png`** · `python examples/05_calibrated_abstention.py`

The same fact as figure 3, now as a frequency over thousands of trials: as sample count
grows, the variance-only SNR climbs to 1281σ while the estimate cloud tightens by 100×
onto the pseudo-true value **−35.5%** - and never approaches the −5% truth. The bias-aware
SNR saturates at 0.165σ and stops.

*Look at:* the estimate distribution shrinking onto the wrong centre as confidence grows.

### 6. ★ An independently developed external simulator produces a phantom fault

**`external_estimate_distribution.png`** · `python examples/06_external_plant_gate.py`

The observer fitted to a **perfectly healthy** PyBaMM cell - an electrochemical simulator
AstraCell did not implement. 500 trials of the capacity estimate cluster tightly around
**−67.6%**, with the truth (0%) far outside a 95% interval only ±0.145% wide. The estimate
is exquisitely precise and grossly wrong: the ECM absorbs unmodelled diffusion as capacity
loss.

*Look at:* the tight red histogram, the green "truth = 0%" line nowhere near it.

### 7. ★ The refusal discriminates

**`positive_control_rates.png`** · `python examples/07_external_positive_control.py`

The v0.4 positive control across five scenarios. With a healthy baseline and a
lack-of-fit gate, AstraCell recovers a real injected fault (true-positive rate 0.94),
weakens a marginal one, and posts **zero false positives on both nulls** - the healthy
cell, and the confounder (a diffusivity change) it cannot express and correctly refuses.

*Look at:* the two red "false positive" bars at exactly 0.00 - the whole point of the
figure.

## Why these seven, and not the other eighteen

The eighteen figures not listed here are supporting detail - parameter-by-parameter
heatmaps, the in-span/out-of-span residual decomposition, the per-excitation overclaim
sweeps. They remain in `reports/figures/` and are referenced by the specialised docs
([CALIBRATION.md](CALIBRATION.md), [EXTERNAL_PLANT.md](EXTERNAL_PLANT.md),
[POSITIVE_CONTROL.md](POSITIVE_CONTROL.md)). They were cut from the public sequence to keep
the argument legible, not because they are weaker - several (e.g. `positive_control_traces`,
the AR(1) `noise_robustness` sweep) are more mechanistically revealing but need more setup
than a public figure should.
